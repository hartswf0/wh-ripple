The Dissertation Statement

“This dissertation argues that generative AI transforms language from a descriptive medium into an operative one by analyzing how text-to-world systems compile navigable environments from verbal instructions.”

The Core Spine: When the output of generative AI shifts from a bounded, two-dimensional image (imagetext) to a navigable space with physical constraints and temporal behavior, the prompt is no longer describing a picture—it is specifying a world. This operative function is called worldtext. The dissertation uses thick prompting as an interpretive method to demonstrate how operative ekphrasis functions across specific text-to-world AI systems, shifting the focus from “better prompts” to the interfaces that make generative outputs stable, inspectable, and revisable.

Practice-Based Research Contributions

Viscosity: A Three-Level Resistance Model
Original Analytic Concept
Viscosity measures the latent space’s resistance to prompt pressure at three levels: Technical (temperature/CFG parameters), Cultural (training distribution’s gravitational pull toward stock aesthetics), and Semantic (concept entanglement—“red brick” is low-viscosity, “brick made of water” is high-viscosity). A thin prompt is a low-viscosity instruction in a high-viscosity medium: the medium wins. A thick prompt matches or exceeds the medium’s resistance. Viscosity is the mechanism that explains why thick prompting works.
Reactive vs. Operative Ekphrasis (Hintze Empirical Finding)
Falsifiable • Repeatable • Dissertation Chapter
Hintze et al. (2025) ran 700 autonomous prompt→image→description→image loops. They converge to 12 generic motifs—“visual elevator music.” When a human is removed from the ekphrastic loop, the system decays to the statistical mean of its training distribution. Reactive ekphrasis = autonomous loop, convergent, authorless. Operative ekphrasis = human-in-loop, divergent, governed. The exact point where human intervention changes trajectory can be measured.
Descriptive Adequacy
Method Standard
What makes a thick prompt “thick” rather than merely verbose? Descriptive adequacy provides the standard: a thick prompt is adequate when it pushes generation away from platform realism in ways answerable to intent, context, code, and consequence. It is inadequate when extra layers are cosmetic. Thick prompting without descriptive adequacy is prompt inflation. Descriptive adequacy without thick prompting is just philosophy.

Annual Progress: Executive Summary

The past year’s work shows a clear trajectory from narrative grammar to spatial compilation, from ethical reflection to ethics infrastructure, from prompt craft to thick prompting, and from media theory to browser-based instruments.

Over the past year, Watson Hartsoe’s work converged around a central research problem: the conversion of description into construction. The year began with experiments in narrative grammar, prompt systems, spatial compilation, ethics, myth, video tools, and generative media workflows. By the end of the year, those experiments consolidated into a defensible dissertation spine: generative AI transforms language from a descriptive medium into an operative one, and thick prompting provides a method for studying that transformation through documented comparison.

The central distilled claim: In generative media, description is no longer only representational. It is operative. It triggers production, establishes constraints, organizes interpretation, and produces media objects that must be evaluated together with the language that generated them.

Dissertation Roadmap

  • 1. From Imagetext to Worldtext
    The problem: What changes when description triggers a navigable environment instead of a frame?
  • 2. The Operative Turn
    The mechanism: How language compiles worlds. Using Wittgenstein (picture theory) and Geertz (thick description).
  • 3. Case Studies: Language Operating
    Demonstration: Close analysis of 3 specific text-to-world AI systems generating environments.
  • 4. Worldtext as Category
    Contribution: What worldtext explains that imagetext, remediation, and simulation do not.

Methodological Limits

  • Scope restriction
    Focusing on text-to-world systems, excluding general LLM chatbots and non-AI worldbuilding.
  • Evidence bounds
    Utilizing bounded cases of documented prompt sequences, avoiding broad platform surveys.
  • Tooling limits
    Excluding prototype development as primary validation to ensure the media theory argument stands on close reading.

Theoretical Spine

  • Mitchell (Imagetext)
    The predecessor. The merged verbal-visual object.
  • Wittgenstein (Picture Theory)
    How language can be operative through arranged relations.
  • Geertz (Thick Description)
    Why surface actions in generated worlds are underdetermined.
  • Bolter (Remediation)
    Writing Space and the genealogy of media transformation.
